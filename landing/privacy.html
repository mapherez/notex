Privacy Policy

How NoteX handles local data, Google Drive backups, and optional AI access.

Your data and account

NoteX stores desktop libraries in local SQLite databases and browser libraries in IndexedDB on your device. Local libraries are kept separate for each Google account.

Desktop use does not require an account. Browser mode requires Google sign-in. NoteX uses your Google account ID, email, display name, and profile image to identify your account and library.

Signing in with Google starts automatic backups on desktop and in the browser. NoteX sends note JSON, library metadata, and attachments directly to the application's hidden data area in your own Google Drive. Google processes this data under its own policies. Your notes and Google tokens are not stored on a NoteX server.

Desktop refresh tokens are stored using the operating system credential store. Browser access tokens are kept in session storage until logout or expiry. Signing out preserves local account libraries; clearing browser site data can remove local notes and pending backups.

The public website and webapp host receive normal HTTP requests and may record connection information in server logs. Google sign-in, Drive access, release checks, and downloads require internet access.

When you start local MCP and connect an AI client, note data requested by that client is sent through the local MCP connection. NoteX does not store an additional copy of that exchange, but the connected AI client may process the content under its own privacy policy.

On desktop, exporting a workspace creates a .notex package with your database and attached files. Exporting one note creates a .notex-note package with that note and its files. You choose where to store or share these packages.

When you import a full-workspace package on desktop, NoteX replaces the current local database and files folder with the selected .notex package.

NoteX does not sell your data or use your notes for advertising. Data is transferred to Google or a connected AI client only through the corresponding features described above.
